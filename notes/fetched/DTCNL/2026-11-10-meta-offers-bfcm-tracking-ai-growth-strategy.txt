⚠️ Meta may add discounts to copied ads
Duplicating an ad for BFCM?
Bram Van der Hallen
spotted a warning when duplicating an ad: Meta may show an offer it found on your site or in another ad, even if you turned promotions off on the original.
Before you duplicate your next batch, go to
Commerce Manager → Settings → Offer merchandising
. If you don’t want Meta pulling in offers automatically, turn off
Detected offers
. Then preview the copy to make sure it shows the deal you intended.

Google’s latest Creative Drop
highlights three ways to get holiday campaigns ready: translate campaigns into 15 languages inside Google Ads, give existing product photos a festive backdrop in Asset Studio, and turn images into video creative with Gemini Omni 1.2.
Still short on BFCM creative? Start with one bestselling product and a few real photos. Try a holiday scene or a new video hook, then check that the product and packaging still look like what customers will actually get.

Search Engine Watch spotted a new pop-up
after shoppers add an item from Google’s free product listings. It explains that items are saved in one cart across Google and that shoppers can get updates like price drops. It was spotted on desktop and mobile, but that doesn’t confirm everyone has it.
See the screenshots in the report. Before BFCM, check that your sale prices and stock in Merchant Center match what shoppers see on your site.

📊 Is your Meta tracking ready for BFCM?
Your BFCM creative might be ready. But is your tracking?
Meta leans on purchase data to measure results and figure out who to show your ads to. So before holiday budgets go up, the Pilothouse Meta team recommends making sure your browser pixel and Conversions API (CAPI) are actually recording purchases correctly.
1. Check browser and server events
First, make sure purchases are coming in through both your pixel and CAPI, and that Meta isn’t counting the same purchase twice.
In Events Manager:
Check that Purchase events are coming in from both
Browser
and
Server
.
When a purchase comes through both, the event name and event ID should match so Meta knows it’s the same order. Every separate purchase should have its own unique ID.
Look at
Diagnostics
for duplicate events, missing parameters, or any other warnings.
2. Compare your purchase events with Shopify orders
Pull your deduplicated Purchase count from Events Manager and your Shopify online-store orders for the same dates and time zone. Pilothouse suggests looking at the last
7, 14, and 28 days
to spot any gaps or sudden changes.
As a rough benchmark,
Pilothouse
aims for Meta to capture
at least 90% of eligible Shopify online-store purchases
across your deduplicated browser and server events. Just leave out test orders and any sales channels your integration doesn’t track, and give delayed events a little time to show up.
If Meta’s number is higher than Shopify’s, look for duplicate events or differences in what each one counts. If it’s lower, check your integration, consent settings, and browser tracking. Some of that gap might just be customers opting out of tracking, and that’s normal.
3. Check purchase values and customer info
Open the Purchase event and look through some sampled activity.
Does the
value
match what your integration is supposed to send?
Is the
currency
right?
Do the
content_ids
match the product or variant IDs in your catalog?
Then check
Event Match Quality
for any missing customer info and Meta’s suggestions for improving it. Make sure your integration formats and hashes things like email and phone the way Meta needs. (Not everything needs to be hashed, though.)
Sampled events are only a spot check, so run a test purchase all the way from checkout to Events Manager too.
And make these checks a regular habit. Catching a missing value or a double-counted purchase now gives you time to fix it before BFCM traffic makes it a much bigger problem.
