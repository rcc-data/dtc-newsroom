💰 Your best ROAS product might not be your most profitable
@MenachemAni
has a good reminder before you set Black Friday budgets. Revenue tells you what sold. It doesn’t tell you what you kept.
Add
cost_of_goods_sold
to your Merchant Center feed
and enable conversions with cart data
to unlock estimated gross-profit reporting. Margin custom labels can then help you group products in PMax.
Before you split up BFCM budget, check which products still make you money after discounts, shipping, and acquisition costs. Just know that adding the fields only gives you the data. It won’t switch PMax to profit-based bidding on its own.
👻 Snapchat is shrinking in North America
Snap’s revenue
grew 14% to $1.72 billion last quarter, but ad revenue only grew 4%.
Globally, Snapchat averaged 495 million daily users, up 4%. In North America, though, it dropped 6% to 92 million.
So if you’re running Snapchat in the US or Canada, check your reach, frequency, and acquisition costs by market before bumping up holiday budgets. Growth somewhere else doesn’t mean there’s more room in your account.

🕯 How ChappyWrap can warm up conversions this holiday season
ChappyWrap started with one blanket, a soft, oversized lamb-patterned throw that Beth Haller LaSala’s family had loved for generations. She couldn’t find anything like it, so she made her own in 2006.
Twenty years later, her daughter Christina Livada runs the company as president and CEO, and it’s an
eight-figure brand that more than doubled its revenue between 2024 and 2025
, according to Entrepreneur.
It’s also very much a
gift brand
, which makes this a pretty good time of year to look at its emails. 🎁 The
Pilothouse
email team went through
three welcome emails
and two abandoned cart emails to find where ChappyWrap could make the offer, the proof, and the next step a little easier to act on.
👋 Welcome flow
What’s working
Email 1 does a lot right. The lifestyle photo is warm, the benefit icons cover the big questions fast (quality, weight, machine washable, giftable), and
the founder letter is signed by Christina herself
. That’s a really personal way to say hello. 💌
What Pilothouse would test
The 15% off code is the reason most people signed up, but you have to scroll to see it. Pilothouse would
move the CTA above the fold
so it’s the first thing subscribers see.
The email could also
borrow the “As Seen In” logos
(People, Martha Stewart, Vogue) from Email 2 and drop them right under the CTA. It’s a quick credibility boost before the founder story.
Email 2 is a great follow-up. It opens with a five-star review (
“I’m never buying any other blanket again”
), puts the CTA early, and has a comparison chart showing how ChappyWrap stacks up on things like machine washability, durability, and gift-ready packaging.
The only thing Pilothouse would test here is
moving the “Don’t forget, you’ve got 15% off” block a little higher,
and maybe pairing it with two or three bestsellers people can tap straight into.
Email 3 has a great headline (“Last Chance to Save”) and a review that’s basically a holiday ad on its own:
“My go-to gift for the special people in my world.”
🎄
The thing to test here is
making the offer as loud as the urgency
. Right now the 15% and the code sit in smaller elements. A top banner with the discount, code, and expiration together (like Email 2) would make it hard to miss.
Same goes for the subject line. “Final Hours to SAVE!” has urgency, but it doesn’t say what you’re saving. Pilothouse would test
“15% Off Ends Tonight,”
as long as it actually does.
👉
Try this on your brand
Open your first welcome email on your phone.
If you can’t see the discount code without scrolling, that’s your first test
.
🛒 Abandoned cart flow
What’s working
“Your cart is calling!”
is a fun headline, and the email keeps the saved blanket and “Return to Cart” button front and center. The path back to checkout is clear. 📞
What Pilothouse would test
Adding a customer photo or product shot next to the review.
It’d show the size and coziness of the blanket in a way text can’t, without pulling attention from the cart.
“You have great taste.”
is a fun follow-up line, and the email keeps the same clean layout as the first one. 😌 There’s room for it to do a different job, though. If someone didn’t come back after the first reminder, something was holding them back.
Pilothouse would test
swapping the New Arrivals and Bestsellers blocks for a review of the saved blanket and a quick proof section
on machine washability and free returns. Two common worries, answered right there.
And with gift season coming,
a third cart email could be worth testing too
. Give it its own job, like answering a common gifting question or testing a small incentive, and track recovered revenue, conversion rate, and unsubscribes.
👉
Try this on your brand
Pull up your first two abandoned cart emails side by side.
If the second one is basically a repeat, give it a new job
.
🧸 Where ChappyWrap goes from here
ChappyWrap’s emails already have the hard stuff down: beautiful product photos, customers who love the brand, and a family story people actually want to read. Pilothouse’s five tests mostly come down to making the next step harder to miss:
Move the welcome CTA and code above the fold
Add the “As Seen In” logos to Welcome Email 1
Make the 15% offer as loud as the urgency
in the last-chance email
Give the second cart email a different job
from the first
Test a third cart email built for gift shoppers
