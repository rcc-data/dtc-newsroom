📊 PMax needs at least 15 conversions a month
PMax performance all over the place? Check how many conversions it’s getting.
Google’s
troubleshooting page
says campaigns need
at least 15 conversions over 30 days
for stable performance. Tracking problems and delays in reporting purchases can also make results look uneven.
Before changing your campaign, check that purchases are being recorded correctly and give delayed conversions time to show up.
Hitting 15 doesn’t guarantee profitable results. It’s a starting point for checking whether Google has enough data before you increase BFCM spend.
📈 Study this strategy before blaming Meta
@PhilKiel
shares how it is possible to scale even when CPMs are up. Some of his biggest accounts increased spend by 21% from September to October, while CPA fell 3% and purchases rose 23%.
His team started scaling early, checked cost caps daily, and tested retention with a holdout before adding budget. The brands built pages to match winning ads and shipped creator samples fast.

🤖 Shopify is testing commissions for AI shopping referrals
This one was buried in last week’s Shopify Edition, but it’s worth pulling out. When an AI assistant helps someone buy a product, the company behind it could get paid.
Shopify is testing a program
that pays participating AI developers
0.3% on eligible purchases they refer
. Brands using Shop Campaigns can fund an extra commission on top.
That extra payment comes with conditions. It applies to eligible US campaigns, and the shopper must complete the purchase in the Shop app or on shop.com.
The program is still an invite-only developer preview. If you use Shop Campaigns, check your external-platform settings and what those commissions would cost you. Shopify hasn’t said whether paying a higher commission affects where your products appear in recommendations.

🔄 Your customer lists need a BFCM refresh
A manually uploaded customer list won’t know who bought on Black Friday unless you refresh it.
That can mean paying to promote something they already own, showing them a discount immediately after they bought it, or sending a “we miss you” message to someone who just returned.
Before holiday traffic picks up, check whether your audiences reflect what customers are doing now.
What to refresh:
Purchaser exclusions:
Add recent buyers to relevant exclusions. Choose the window around your product’s repurchase cycle, and keep useful cross-sell offers separate.
Retargeting audiences:
Remove converted shoppers from campaigns promoting the purchase they just completed.
Lookalike seeds:
Review customer quality alongside recency. Purchase value, repeat behavior, and other relevant signals can help you choose a useful seed.
Lapsed customers:
Remove shoppers who have returned so re-engagement campaigns stay relevant.
Advertising permissions:
Update audience membership when customers withdraw applicable permissions. An email unsubscribe and an advertising opt-out aren’t necessarily the same instruction.
Put these refreshes on your calendar:
Pilothouse
recommends treating these as checkpoints. When orders spike, refresh manual lists more often.
Automate the upkeep
Where your tools support it, use customer-audience integrations to keep membership current. Rolling purchaser audiences can also help maintain exclusions, though tracking coverage and matching affect how complete they are.
Check that syncs are running, audience sizes look plausible, and exclusions are actually applied to the intended campaigns.
You don’t need to exclude every customer from every campaign. The goal is to match the next message to their latest purchase, whether that means giving them breathing room or showing them a relevant second product.

🎧 Your BFCM offer isn’t enough for cold traffic
A 30% discount lands differently with someone who already knows your brand than with someone discovering it for the first time.
On the latest DTC Rundown episode, Jacob and Abby from Pilothouse’s Meta team break down why brands should
think differently about BFCM creative for cold and retargeting audiences
.
For someone who already knows your products, the offer can do most of the work. Clear, bold creative that puts the discount front and center may be exactly what gets them to finally buy.
Cold traffic still needs more convincing.
They’re seeing discounts everywhere, and 30% off doesn’t tell them why your cookware, skincare, or apparel deserves their attention. Your creative still needs strong hooks, product education, and enough variety to introduce the brand while making the BFCM offer impossible to miss.
That means you probably shouldn’t be running the exact same BFCM ads to everyone.
Jacob and Abby also get into how they’re using video to diversify creative, when brands should start their sales this year, and why Cyber Monday shouldn’t automatically signal the end of your holiday push.
