🛍️ Google’s Promotion Mode gives your sale more room to reach shoppers
Google’s Promotion Mode
is in open beta and lets eligible Search and Performance Max campaigns temporarily lower their target return on ad spend for sales lasting 3 to 14 days.
That gives Google room to bid on searches it might pass up under your usual target, helping you reach more shoppers and potentially pick up more orders while the offer is live.
You can also add a daily budget where supported, and Google restores your previous settings when the sale ends.
Before switching it on for BFCM, work out the lowest return you can afford after discounts and shipping. If you already use Smart Bidding Exploration, check both settings, since Google adds the two allowances together.
🤖 Walmart teams up with Muse. Amazon blocks it.
Back in September, Amazon blocked Muse from accessing its platform.
Walmart is going the opposite route
. The ecommerce giant is partnering with Meta’s shopping assistant.
Why should brands care? If a shopper asks Muse to pick a product, they may never see the sponsored listings you paid for. Amazon says it hasn’t seen a meaningful impact on its ad business from outside assistants so far.
If you sell on Walmart, try asking Muse the questions shoppers ask about your category. See which products it recommends and whether it gets your price, sizes, and availability right.
🍬 Sour Patch Kids’ maker sues Joyride
Mondelēz filed a trademark lawsuit against Joyride
, the candy brand backed by YouTuber Ryan Trahan. It alleges Joyride’s Sour Smacks look too much like Sour Patch Kids, pointing to the kid-shaped gummies and yellow-and-green packaging.
@GivnerAriel
spotted something interesting. Mondelēz cites Trahan’s own videos about trying to overtake Sour Patch Kids on Instagram. The posts were about social strategy, and Mondelēz is using them to support its case about the candy and packaging. Those claims are still allegations.
If you’re building a challenger brand, look at your packaging next to the category leader’s before it goes to print. What would tell a shopper at a glance that it’s yours?
🎬 Meta can help your creators turn ideas into a shot list
Meta updated its Creator Assistant
with alerts for trends in your niche, feedback on drafts, and suggested replies to comments.
It can also turn an idea into a storyboard and shot list, and it’s now available on the web in Creator Dashboard.
Working with a creator on a holiday demo? If they have Creator Assistant, give them the product, the offer, and the one thing shoppers need to see. They can use it to draft a shot list for your review.

🍴 DTC Dines: Vancouver, the debrief
Last week we brought DTC Dines back to Vancouver for a second time, and it was our biggest dinner yet. Just under 40 founders and operators joined us at Acquafarina, a beautiful high-ceilinged Italian spot in downtown Van, across three tables that I bounced between all night. We opened with champagne, and things stayed bubbly all night.
Guests I talked to were surprised and delighted that from the moment they arrived, the only objective was organic conversation with their contemporaries…no speeches or ads to slow things down.

Attribution came up at every table, especially for brands juggling retail, wholesale and Amazon alongside DTC. Rising Meta costs have pushed many people toward retention, with a lot of interest in email, SMS and subscriptions for consumable brands, and a few people traded notes on TikTok Shop.

💡 3 weeks left to Black Friday: Surface your bundle builder
Each week leading up to Black Friday, we’re sharing one relatively simple CRO test operators can launch before Q4 traffic peaks. These tests are designed to uncover quick conversion wins while there is still time to apply the results.
Bachan’s already offers a Mix & Match builder that lets customers combine its barbecue sauces and dips. The problem is visibility.
The builder is featured on its own product page, but customers browsing Bachan’s high-traffic collection pages may never discover it.
Pilothouse
recommends adding a sticky banner to the top of Bachan’s collection pages so more shoppers encounter the Mix & Match builder during product discovery.
The current message, “The more you mix, the more you save,” communicates a general benefit. Pilothouse suggests using more direct language that explains the action and reward:
“Build Your Custom 4-Pack. Free Shipping on $35+.”
This could send more shoppers into the bundle flow, increase the number of products added per order, and help customers reach Bachan’s $35 free-shipping threshold.
How to run this CRO test:
Add the sticky banner to one high-traffic collection page. Link the banner directly to the Mix & Match builder.
Keep the product assortment, prices, and sorting order unchanged.
Split traffic on the same collection page between the current layout and the sticky banner. Use
completed bundle purchases per collection-page visitor
as the primary metric.
Track bundle completion rate, units per order, AOV, and total conversion.
Confirm the banner does not cover navigation or product content on mobile.
This test uses an offer and shopping tool Bachan’s has already built. The change is simply giving more customers a chance to find it.

🎧 They sent 75% fewer emails. Revenue went up.
Sending more emails should mean more chances to generate revenue. Right?
One brand discovered the opposite was true.
Before the Pilothouse email team stepped in, the brand was sending heavily while its
click-through rate had fallen to roughly 0.2%
.
After a rewarm and a major reduction in send volume, September email sends were approximately 75% lower, yet the brand generated nearly its
highest number of real clicks all year
.
Revenue increased too.
Then came an interesting second test.
In October, the brand roughly doubled its email volume again. Real clicks fell around 20%, and inbox placement started slipping.

TWBERP
