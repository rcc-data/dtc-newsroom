Key Takeaways
The top 6 LLMs (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Grok, Perplexity, DeepSeek) now drive 76B sessions a month worldwide, and YoY growth has hit 37% worldwide and 45% in the US.
Claude has the largest relative growth, with +1901% worldwide and +862% in the US. However, ChatGPT still has an 81.2% share worldwide and 80.7% in the US.
ChatGPT recently announced 1.2B weekly active users.
Despite the growth of Claude, ChatGPT has regained market share worldwide since June 2026, increasing it by +0.8 percentage points from June to September.
AI hit a surprising and little-reported plateau between July and December 2025 worldwide, growing only 2.3%, while the US grew 10% over the same window.
Growth has since rebounded, with a 19% increase since January 2026 worldwide and 20% in the US.
86% of AI usage worldwide happens in-app, 74% in the US. Adding mobile web takes it to 89% worldwide and 78% in the US.
Referral traffic from AI continues to climb, with 92% YoY growth worldwide. ChatGPT owns 60% of referral traffic, followed by Gemini at 14%, and Claude at 10%.
Google Search continues to grow and converge with AI, with 64% of Google Searches showing AI Overviews.
This is an updated version of our previous study, “
AI Is Much Bigger Than You Think
” published in March 2026. We share new data and compare it against our previous study.

The Messy & Unpredictable Growth of AI
Forecasts for new technologies often predict a conservative linear growth curve or a more ambitious exponential hockey stick growth curve. Most forecasts of AI favor the more ambitious hockey stick.
AI grew exponentially between July 2023 and June 2025, plateaued from July to December 2025, and has grown steadily since January 2026. However, on a micro level, no one LLM has followed the forecasted clean, predictable growth curves. The reality is instead a series of unpredictable events, bursts, and ups and downs.

Something Big Is Happening with Claude
Starting in February 2026, Claude has seen one of the largest growth trajectories among LLMs.
US sessions are up +252%, between January and March 2026, from 90M to 317M.
Daily active users (DAU) grew by 345%, and app installs by 1192%, comparing the end of March 2026 to the beginning of January 2026.
Worldwide, the numbers are even larger. Sessions increased 296%, from 459M to 1.82B, DAU by 443%, and app installs by 1495%.
Claude's growth is not a temporary spike caused solely by controversy in the United States. Rather, the surge appears to be driven by a combination of three catalysts: the launch of Claude Cowork, the release of Opus and Sonnet 4.6, and a widely publicized standoff with the Pentagon, which introduced Claude to a much larger audience.

The AI Attribution Gap
Referral traffic from AI is a small channel relative to others. Our data suggest that AI accounts for less than 1% of traffic on average to websites.
Relative Traffic Volume by Channel (US, Q2 2026)
Source:
Channel traffic: Total, Organic, Paid and GenAI. Similarweb.
However, referral traffic does not tell the full story of the impact from AI.
It is common for AI answers to have little to nothing to click on, even when products and brands are recommended. Anecdotally, many users who see a brand or product in AI will open a new tab and navigate directly to or search for the brand in search engines, then visit the website. This traffic is then misattributed to direct or branded search, respectively.
Our June 2026
case study with n8n
evaluated the attribution gap between conversions from AI referral traffic and self-reported conversions. We found that conversions from AI referral visits accounted for only 10% of conversions.
We do not have sufficient data to draw broader market conclusions, but this one case study suggests that referral traffic from AI undercounts its total impact.

Comparison with Prior Research
This study builds on a similar study we published in March 2026, “
AI Is Much Bigger Than You Think
.” This study was one of the first to show the growth in AI usage on mobile apps. This study is mostly consistent with the previous study, yet we call out several differences.
Shortcomings in our prior research are worth noting:
AI vs. Search
Our original study compared the size of search-related AI with search engines.
The previous study included web visits for the top 6 search engines and both web and mobile app sessions for the top 5 LLMs.
We did not include mobile app sessions for search engines. Most search engines receive little usage of their respective mobile apps.
However, Google's Android app is heavily used, with an estimated 11.8B monthly sessions in the US.
The Google Android app is used for a variety of non-search-related applications such as image recognition, translation, and Google Discover, as well as traditional Google search. We have no reliable data source for how many sessions are focused on search or not. For this reason, we can no longer reliably compare the relative size of AI for search vs. search engines.
Our previous study likely undercounted Google search usage from Google's Android mobile app and, therefore, search-related AI usage relative to search engines.
AI Visibility & Branded Search
Many AI answers that suggest products or brands do not have a strong call to click. Oftentimes, when users see a product, they search for the brand in a search engine and visit the brand site.
This can inflate the number of branded searches in search engines.
Early analysis suggests that traffic from search engines via branded search queries is relatively flat YoY indicating that AI growth has not produced a net increase in branded search traffic. We will share the full analysis in an upcoming study.
However, at a micro level, we have evidence suggesting that some search engine traffic is caused by AI visibility. As we showed in our
study with n8n
, 90% of conversions from AI were not accounted for via last-touch attribution.
We do not have reliable data or studies evaluating how much of search engine usage is caused by AI visibility. However, we acknowledge that the usage of search engines may be overcounted as a result of this.
Data & Methodology
Definition of Metrics
Visits
— A “visit” to a website is counted when a visitor accesses one or more pages during a session. Subsequent pageviews are included in the same visit until the user is inactive for more than 30 minutes. If a user becomes active again after 30 minutes, that counts as a new visit. A new session will also start at midnight.
Session (Apps)
— A session is a period of time when a user interacts with an app. Session metrics are a primary measure of user engagement, as they capture how frequently and how long users interact with an app.
Web Visits vs. App Sessions
— Web “visits” are session counts with a 30-minute inactivity rule aggregated monthly, while app “sessions” reflect real app usage periods and are typically measured and analyzed on a daily engagement basis.
Monthly Visits (Web)
measure how many times users visit a website. A “visit” (or web session) begins when someone loads a page and ends after
30 minutes of inactivity
or at
midnight
. The metric simply sums all of these sessions over the course of a month.
App Sessions
measure how often users actively use a mobile app. A session represents a period of interaction while the app is open and in use. Instead of a fixed 30-minute timeout rule in the documentation, app sessions are based on actual app engagement and are typically reported as
sessions per user per day
, along with average and total session time.
Gen AI Referral Traffic
— A website visit that originated from a Gen AI product including ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, DeepSeek, and others.
Desktop Apps
Since our previous study, both the
Claude
and
OpenAI
desktop apps have grown significantly. We do not have a reliable data source for desktop apps. Therefore, the size of AI usage and the % of AI that is used on desktop vs. mobile will both be undercounted.
