🎨 Meta’s grading your creative variety
Are your ads different, or just versions of the same thing?
@Peter_Quadrel
pointed out Meta’s Creative Diversity column. You can find it under Columns → Customize columns → search “diversity.”
It rates ad sets High, Medium, or Low by comparing images and video thumbnails. He also points out that two very different videos could score Low if their thumbnails look alike.
He’s using it next to hook rate, hold rate, and spend allocation. So look at the rating, then check performance before you start swapping out creative.

Good news for whoever runs your Instagram account.

🚚 Your Black Friday deal can fall apart at shipping
According to
DHL’s new Black Friday findings
, one in three Black Friday shoppers say choosing shipping is where they’re most likely to bail on a purchase. Nine in ten care most about fast, free delivery and easy returns.
Before traffic picks up, make shipping costs, delivery dates, and return terms easy to find on your product pages and in the cart. Nobody wants to get all the way to checkout to find out the deal doesn’t work for them.

🧒 Hiya makes kids’ vitamins parents feel good about. Here’s what Pilothouse would test next.
Hiya Health sells chewable kids’ vitamins with zero added sugar, formulated by pediatricians, and shipped on a refill subscription. You pick how many kids you’re buying for, and the first box comes with a refillable glass bottle and a sheet of stickers.
There’s a lot Hiya gets right already:
The product story is strong.
Zero added sugar, pediatrician-formulated, and a glass bottle you keep refilling. That gives Hiya a lot more to talk about than the average kids’ vitamin.
The site feels like it’s made for families.
The imagery is built around kids, so parents know right away who it’s for.
The subscription fits how families buy.
Choosing by number of kids is a smart way to set up the order.
The cart already recommends other products
, so there’s a base to build on.
Pilothouse
’s CRO team went through Hiya’s site and pulled together five tests aimed at one thing, helping parents feel sure about what they’re signing up for.
Where Hiya goes from here
1. Make the zero-sugar difference obvious
Zero added sugar is one of Hiya’s biggest selling points, and right now parents have to dig for it a bit. The homepage hero leads with the offer.
Pilothouse would test a version that shows the difference up top:
Headline:
The Zero-Added-Sugar Vitamin Made for Kids
Subheadline:
One daily chewable with 15 essential vitamins and minerals, formulated by pediatricians and delivered in a refillable glass bottle.
Visual:
A side-by-side comparison of a gummy vitamin and Hiya’s 0g-added-sugar chewable. Hiya says some other children’s gummy vitamins contain up to 5g of added sugar per serving.
Under the button, add the review line, something like “4.9 out of 5 from 7,000+ reviews.”
Track CTA clicks, how many people make it to the product page, and completed subscriptions.
2. Put the renewal price right next to the offer
The product page shows $22.50, “Reg $45,” and “50% OFF TODAY.” There’s a footnote saying the discount is first month only, but it’s not next to the button.
Pilothouse would move that info up, with a line under the price like this:
$22.50 today. Renews at $45 every 30 days. Cancel by email or in your customer portal.
Parents know exactly what they’re paying next month before they click, and that’s one less reason to hesitate.
3. Move the pediatrician proof up to the button
Hiya already says the vitamins are pediatrician-formulated. On mobile, that sits pretty far from where parents actually decide.
Pilothouse would test a small “Pediatrician Formulated” badge right above the CTA, linked to a short page about the experts and how the formula was built.
4. Help parents build the routine in the cart
A parent buying the Kids Daily Multivitamin might also be thinking about the Probiotic or Bedtime Essentials. Hiya already recommends products in the cart, so the next test is making those picks feel like part of a routine.
Pilothouse would test a “Complete the Routine” module with three products, a one-line reason for each, and a one-click add:
Kids Daily Probiotic:
for digestion, alongside the daily multivitamin
Kids Daily Greens + Superfoods:
55 whole-food sourced ingredients for digestion, growth, and brain health
Kids Daily Iron+:
for development and brain health, easy on the stomach
Bonus points if the add-ons carry over the number-of-kids selection. Watch average order value, checkout completion, and cart abandonment to make sure it’s helping.
5. Take the taste risk off the table
Every parent has bought something their kid refused to touch. A guarantee could take that worry away before the first order.
One idea Pilothouse would explore:
“The Picky Eater Guarantee: If your kids don’t love the taste, your first month is on us. Easily snooze or cancel future orders right from your phone.”
Test it near the CTA or the cart’s checkout button. Along with conversion, keep an eye on refund requests, support tickets, and repeat orders so you know what it costs.
👉 Try this on your brand
Go through your own subscription checkout like a first-time customer. Before you hit the button, could you say what you’ll pay next month, why your product beats the cheaper option, and how to cancel? If any of those take a scroll or a footnote, start there.
