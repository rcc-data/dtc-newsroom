💳 ChatGPT can now pick up the bill
OpenAI just launched
ChatGPT Wallet
, so its agent can now buy stuff for you, not just find it. For now it’s only for eligible US Pro subscribers using its personal agents.
Shoppers still have to OK every purchase with something like Face ID, and their card details stay hidden behind payment tokens.
It plugs into normal checkout flows, so in theory you don’t have to do anything. But if your site or fraud tools block the agent or decline the card, that sale’s gone. Might be worth a quick check with whoever runs your fraud settings.
🪤 Great blended ROAS? Check before scaling
Say it’s Black Friday, sales are flying, and your blended ROAS looks incredible. Time to crank up Meta spend, right?
Not so fast, says
@RokHladnik
. A lot of that could be coming from returning customers, email, SMS, and people who were just waiting for your sale. Your overall numbers can look great while every extra dollar on acquisition is getting less efficient.
So before you bump budgets, he says to watch two things:
New-customer CAC as spend goes up
How much new-customer revenue that extra spend is bringing in
And look back at your past BFCM cohorts. Sale shoppers don’t always come back as often, so your usual LTV math might be a little generous.
(We’ve got more on scaling the right way in the Pilothouse piece below.)

🚀 Are you ready to scale spend for BFCM?
It’s tempting to crank budgets way up for Black Friday. And sure, it can pay off. But big budget jumps or major edits can throw ad sets back into the learning phase, which is the last thing you want on the busiest weekend of the year.
So the
Pilothouse Meta team
put together a plan.
1️⃣ Start turning it up early
Don’t wait until Black Friday morning. Start raising budgets gradually in the week or two before, as long as your purchase CPA, store sales, and margins can handle it.
After each bump, give it a minute. Check how things look before you add more. And try not to change your budget, targeting, and creative all at once, or you won’t know what’s working.
If your sale kicks off early, time your increases with the offer and the demand you’re seeing.
2️⃣ Get your Black Friday spend scheduled
Build your sale campaigns now, get them through review, and schedule them to go live with your offer. (Quick sanity check: make sure you know what time zone your ad account is in.)
For a dedicated Black Friday campaign, a lifetime budget lets you cap spend over a set period. Start it at midnight, end it at 11:59 p.m., and you’ve got an
almost 24-hour window
. Just keep an eye on delivery, because a lifetime budget doesn’t guarantee Meta will spend the whole thing.
And heads up, Meta has two scheduling tools that sound similar. Dayparting decides
when
your ads run. Budget scheduling lets eligible accounts plan budget increases ahead of time.
3️⃣ Pick how you want to scale
There are two main ways to go, and it depends on how your account’s doing and what you want to learn.
Vertical scaling
is putting more money behind the campaigns that are already winning, then watching whether they stay efficient as spend goes up.
Horizontal scaling
is testing new campaigns with a different offer, market, or creative angle. Just know that copying a winning campaign won’t automatically get you in front of new people.
Pilothouse’s advice is to test new campaign structures before peak whenever you can. A campaign getting approved early doesn’t mean it’s figured out how to deliver yet.
Testing attribution settings too? Compare both versions with the same attribution reporting window.
Plan your increases now, then let performance tell you how much more to add as the week goes on.

🎧 How to avoid the Google Ads BFCM hangover
Your Black Friday campaigns are crushing it, conversions are rolling in, and everything looks great.
Then the sale ends, performance drops off a cliff, and your Google Ads account takes forever to bounce back.
On the latest AKNF, Pilothouse Google strategist Zavire joins Eric to talk about
why the post-BFCM hangover happens and what you can do to soften it
.
Part of it is just the urgency disappearing once the offer’s gone. But a lot of it comes from all the changes advertisers make before, during, and after BFCM. When you switch up budgets, offers, creative, and landing pages all at once, performance shifts and it’s hard to tell what caused it.
Zavire shares a few ways to prepare:
Forecast your conversion rate.
Use past promo data to estimate how much more likely visitors are to buy during your sale.
Be picky with seasonality adjustments.
Save them for short sales where you expect a big conversion-rate spike beyond your normal seasonal patterns.
Give changes time.
Adjust budgets and ROAS targets based on performance, then wait out conversion delays before judging the results.
Plan past Cyber Monday.
Keep the right campaigns running into December to catch the rest of the holiday demand.
