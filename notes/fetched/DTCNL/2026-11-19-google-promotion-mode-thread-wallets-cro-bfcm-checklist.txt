🚀 8 days left until Black Friday: Can shoppers understand your homepage in five seconds?
We’re sharing quick CRO tests operators can still launch before Q4 traffic peaks. At this point, the goal is to make the traffic you’re already paying for easier to convert, without rebuilding your site.
This week we’re looking at benefit-first hero copy.
Thread Wallets’ current homepage headline is “New Prints, Something for Everyone.” It tells shoppers there’s variety. Someone landing there for the first time may still need another beat to figure out why they’d want a Thread wallet over the one in their pocket.
Pilothouse would test that against a headline that leads with the product benefit:
“Slim carry goods for everyday essentials.”
The supporting copy can then explain the value a little further, and the CTA moves from the generic “Shop Now” to something more directional like “Shop Best Sellers.”
Why test it?
Black Friday can bring a lot of first-time visitors to your homepage. Those shoppers may be comparing several brands at once, so your hero has a pretty small window to answer a few basic questions:
What makes this different?
Why is it useful?
Where should I click next?
Clearer copy could help more people see the value before they bounce or start hunting around the page.
For the test, keep the imagery and the rest of the homepage the same. Run the new headline, supporting copy, and CTA as one hero treatment against the current version.
Primary metric:
Hero CTA click-through rate
Secondary metrics:
Homepage bounce rate and conversion rate
👉 Try this on your brand
Open your homepage on your phone and give yourself five seconds. If you didn’t already know the brand, would you understand why you should care?

🚨 Your BFCM checklist: 9 things to check now
Are you ready for the rush? 👀
Here’s a few small (and easy) things to check before traffic spikes.
Pilothouse’s email team
pulled together nine last-minute checks across your sends, storefront, and abandoned-cart setup so you’re getting every last drop from the list you’ve been keeping warm and the new audience you’re bringing in.
📧 Before you send
1. Clean your lists.
Exclude spam complaints, bounced addresses, and unsubscribed contacts so you’re protecting deliverability before volume goes up.
2. Check how newer subscribers perform.
Split your established sending segment into contacts created within the past 365 days and older contacts. See whether one group responds differently before you send everyone the same thing.
3. Plan your follow-ups now.
Second and third sends can work, especially for engaged shoppers who clicked and didn’t buy. Exclude anyone who purchases between sends and check for overlap with automated flows.
4. Stick with segments you trust.
This probably isn’t the weekend to introduce a completely new audience strategy. Use the segments you already understand.
5. Make the offer obvious in the subject line.
If there’s ever a time to be clear about the discount, it’s Black Friday. Test that against your usual subject-line style.
🛒 Before shoppers hit the site
6. Test every discount code.
Check eligible products, minimum spends, exclusions, discount combinations, and any restrictions you didn’t mean to leave on.
7. Add a sitewide sale banner.
Someone landing on a PDP, blog post, or collection page should still be able to find the BFCM offer immediately.
🛍️ Before they abandon
8. Check your abandoned-cart timing.
Pilothouse suggests testing reminders around 20 minutes, eight hours, and 24 hours.
9. Update the abandoned-cart emails themselves.
Make the BFCM offer easy to see, while keeping the main CTA pointed back to the shopper’s cart or checkout. A separate banner can send people to the broader sale collection.

Run through your own BFCM journey from inbox to checkout. Open the email, click the offer, test the code, abandon the cart, and see what happens next.
